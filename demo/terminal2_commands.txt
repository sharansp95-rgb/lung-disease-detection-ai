WHAT TO EXPLAIN
====================================================================

Say this (X-ray):
  "This runs the served X-ray model against 1,922 test images it never
  touched during training or checkpoint selection."
  Point at: "TEST accuracy: 0.9657"
  "96.57%, broken down per class -- not hidden behind one average, so you
  can see Tuberculosis, only 3% of the training data, still has 100%
  recall. Through the live website, with every guard active and every
  rejection counted as an error, the same 1,922 images score 91.05%; the
  gap comes from the lung-crop step, and finding it is one of our results."

Say this (CT):
  Point at: "TEST accuracy: 0.9806"
  "98.06% on 103 test slices -- after the leakage fix from Terminal 1. The
  only two errors are Benign slices predicted Normal; with just 12 Benign
  test images, each error moves Benign recall by 8 points."

Be upfront about CT if asked about real-world use:
  "This number is in-distribution. On CT slices from other hospitals the
  model fails -- it labelled none of the external Normal slices as Normal.
  We traced that to the model learning one dataset's acquisition style, and
  we report it as a limitation; the CT route is a proof of concept."

On Grad-CAM (open the CT grids):
  "A model can get the right answer for the wrong reason. Grad-CAM shows
  which regions drove each decision, which is how we caught the CT
  shortcut in the first place."

One-line summary if asked "why not just say 98% and move on":
  "Because per-class numbers, the live-route number and the external test
  are what tell you how far the 98% can actually be trusted."
